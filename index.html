About Me

I'm a Ph.D. student in the College of Engineering at the University of Georgia, currently visiting the LIDAR Lab at the Georgia Institute of Technology. I work on robot learning and embodied AI.

I study how learned controllers can make reliable decisions when observations are incomplete or physical conditions change. My dissertation focuses on action decoding, recovery without losing existing capabilities, and active tactile sensing for humanoid control. I am also exploring how these ideas apply to autonomous scanning-probe measurements.

When is the current observation sufficient to act, and when is another physical measurement worth taking?

My dissertation studies how a learned controller should choose an action from a policy's predictions, add a recovery behavior while preserving prior successes, and use touch to improve contact decisions in humanoid loco-manipulation.

I am exploring autonomous scanning-probe microscopy as a related application. The first proposed study is to test whether a workflow can locate a bowtie structure on a reference sample, choose the next scan location, and obtain a usable scan. I plan to evaluate scan quality, human interventions, and unnecessary rescans.

Two proposed experiments ask whether an extra measurement improves the next decision: predicting a nickelate film's response to a write pulse, and locating a semiconductor interface by choosing where to scan or when to change excitation energy.

PersonaRobotBench

A benchmark for evaluating whether robot policies infer a user's preferences and carry them out. Across 100 everyday tasks and 217 preference variants, the benchmark evaluates preference inference and physical execution separately. A simulated Unitree G1 starts from the same scene and receives the same instruction across variants, allowing comparisons across target preferences under matched starting conditions.

Faster imitation by re-timing demonstrations within the controller limit

Under review

A policy trained on human teleoperation inherits the operator's pace. Speeding up the demonstrations works only until success collapses at 2–3×. I show the collapse has a mechanical cause: merged commands ask for more displacement per control period than the low-level controller can deliver, and the share of such pairs in the training data predicts where the retrained policy fails. The fix is a one-parameter rule that merges consecutive commands only while their sum stays realizable. It keeps the demonstrated path and speeds up only where the demonstrator left slack.

120 → 93 sreal-arm pick-and-place, 22.5% faster than the demonstration
≤ 4 mmgrasp-point error on hardware, vs. 8 cm for uniform re-timing
96% vs 60%success on Robomimic Can, vs. uniform 2× speed-up
71% @ 2.3×of 429 clips on a G1 whole-body tracker (uniform: 51% @ 2×)

Sequential recovery updates for learned humanoid controllers

Under review

A competent learned controller already recovers from many disturbances, so improving it means learning a correction and also learning when not to apply it. Freezing a skill's parameters does not settle the second part: in a controller that routes among frozen skills, a new entry rule can take over states an old skill already handled. Each update therefore searches for a feedback skill at a measured failure and fits an entry rule that rejects every state on an accumulated successful trajectory. The skill is admitted only if the composed controller makes the new recovery and replays every protected trajectory exactly.

+62recoveries over 192 withheld perturbations, two updates on each of four streams
0base-controller successes lost
8protected trajectories broken when the constraint is removed

Correction Fidelity and Timing for Frozen Policies under Actuation Interface Faults

This work follows up on Efference-Copy Adaptation (ECA), which used prediction errors from a model of the healthy system to correct a frozen policy's commands online. We focus on actuator interface faults and ask whether recovery fails because the correction starts too late or because the command sent at that time is inaccurate.

We replay trials with the simulator's exact inverse at the controller's intervention time and at a reference time to measure the two effects. For Humanoid channel permutations, delaying exact correction by eight steps reduced recoveries from 16 to 3 out of 16. Evidence-Triggered Input Compensation (ETIC) scores a fixed library of candidate faults against observed actuator responses and applies the best-supported inverse. On Reacher double sign reversals, selecting a single inverse raised recovery from 20 to 43 out of 43 compared with averaging candidate corrections.

495/528recoveries across four simulated tasks for faults in the candidate library, versus 30/528 without correction and 509/528 with the immediate exact inverse
143/144fault-free trajectories reproduced exactly

Heterogeneous recurrence for partially observed control

Memory architectures for partially observed control are usually ranked by average return, without asking whether their inductive bias matches what the observation hides. On masked MuJoCo it matters: a structured prior–innovation recurrence beats a GRU when the hidden variable is a derivative of the observation, but not when it is an integral. DuoCell splits one recurrent state budget between the two constructions and lets the policy weigh both.

+0.456normalized IQM over a tuned TransformerXL in the derivative regime, on held-out seeds
5×training budget, where the lead holds and widens

Follow-up work asks which distinctions a memory with a fixed bit budget must preserve for a later control decision, and how the best encoding changes when the controller can obtain another measurement.

Structure Follows Uncertainty: Cyclic State Estimation and Control under Partial Observation

This follow-up extends cyclic counting to state estimation with noisy and missing observations. When an increment is observed exactly, a phase rotation advances the state. When it is uncertain, a cyclic filter assigns probability to several possible shifts and revises its belief when a measurement arrives. A shared planner uses these estimates to test how belief quality changes the decision to sense again.

5.839 to 0.966 natsfinal-state negative log likelihood in a simulated valve with 1% token misreads; matched Bayes reached 0.965 nats
20.2° to 12.8°120-second heading error on BROAD when a bias state is added to phase rotation
12.33% to 9.04%gait-phase RMSE as a share of a cycle, comparing LSTM with the cyclic filter with three-second sensor dropouts on Camargo

With the same planner and simulated valve observations, the token-constrained cyclic filter achieved 63.0 utility points versus 62.3 for GRU and 63.3 for matched Bayes in the default setting. The learned filter matched or exceeded GRU across 75 settings, with a positive paired bootstrap interval in 47.

Co-optimizing vehicle dynamics and powertrain for connected electric vehicles

ACC 2025

Eco-driving saves the most energy when vehicle speed and powertrain operation are optimized together. For a dual-motor electric vehicle, I built control-oriented models of the vehicle dynamics and the electric powertrain. These turn speed planning and front/rear torque allocation into one optimal control problem that is solved in real time with CasADi-based nonlinear MPC, using predicted traffic and signal timing. I validated it in a VISSIM simulation of a real signalized corridor in Chattanooga, TN, calibrated with real-world data.

12.8–24.5%less battery state-of-charge depletion than the preceding vehicle, across four cases with ideal traffic predictions
3.2–4.4%lower traction power with optimized front/rear motor torque allocation than with a fixed split
< 1 saverage solve time for a 15 s prediction horizon
